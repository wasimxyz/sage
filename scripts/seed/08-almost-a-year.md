---
title: Almost a year
date: 2026-09-09
---

Can't sleep, so I'm writing. I was making dinner tonight with a random playlist on, and the song from the Michigan trip came on. The one Marissa played four times in a row on the drive up to Sleeping Bear, until I begged her to pick anything else, and she laughed and played it a fifth time. I stood at the stove holding a wooden spoon and didn't move until it was over.

I did the math after. It'll be a year in about five weeks. We were together five years, and a year ago she sat on the edge of our bed and said she'd felt like my roommate for a long time, and that she thought I felt the same way. I remember being angry that she said it like it was obvious. I think I was angry because it was.

The thing is, she'd been telling me for a whole year before that. Not in those words. She'd bring up the future, like whether we were going to get engaged, or whether we should stop renting and buy a place. Every time, I said some version of "after this project." After the clinic launch. After the rebrand. After the holidays. I wasn't lying, exactly. There was always a project. But I think I knew even then that I was using work to put off deciding.

So the guilt is the part I can't shake. She didn't leave out of nowhere. She asked, and asked, and I kept saying "later" until later turned into her packing boxes. When people say "it just didn't work out," I nod along, but that's not really what happened. I let it not work out.

The weird part is I don't think I want her back. I'm pretty sure I don't. We were good for a long time, and then we were just polite, and polite is its own kind of lonely. When I picture calling her, I don't picture us getting back together. I picture her asking how I'm doing, and me actually knowing the answer.

Maybe what I miss is the life around her. Sunday mornings at the farmers market, where she'd make me carry the heavy bag and tell me it counted as a workout. Dinner at her sister Elena's place every Sunday night, where everyone talked over each other and somebody always cried laughing. Thursday trivia at the Owl with Jason and the crew, where Marissa was weirdly good at geography. My week had a whole shape to it, and she was in the middle of it. When she left I didn't just lose her. I lost the shape.

After she moved out, I stayed in our apartment for nine more months. I told myself it was the lease. Really, I didn't know where else to go. Every street in Logan Square had something on it. The taco place. The bench outside the bookstore. The community woodshop on Milwaukee Ave where I built our table.

I told everyone I took the Seattle job because it was a great opportunity, and it was. A senior role, more money, a team building something millions of people use. But the recruiter emailed me on a Tuesday, I said yes by the next Monday, and I barely asked any questions. If I'm honest, the timing wasn't an accident. I figured if I moved far enough away, I'd get to start over clean.

Except I brought myself with me. Same guy, new zip code, and now nobody around who knew him before.

Going to try to sleep. Still haven't called Jason. He's texted twice this week. Tomorrow.
