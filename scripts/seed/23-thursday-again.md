---
title: Thursday, again
date: 2026-10-22
---

Went to trivia at the Pinewood with Dev and his roommate Sam. It's a narrow bar with fake wood paneling and a quizmaster named Lou who wears a bow tie and plays a kazoo whenever someone gets an answer wrong. We came in last out of eleven teams. Dead last. We got a single point in the music round, and only because Sam recognized the theme song from a cartoon.

I had a great time. I don't remember the last time I laughed that much with people I've known for less than a month. Sam is loud, confident, and wrong about almost everything, and Dev keeps a running list on his phone of every answer Sam has gotten wrong with total confidence. Our team name was "The Espresso, Technically," after Dev's mug. Lou mispronounced it every single time.

Somewhere in the second round I realized I'd stopped performing. I wasn't doing the voice. Nobody asked "why Seattle" or "how's the move." Sam asked what I do, and I said I design sign-up screens for a budgeting app. He said, "Oh, so you're why I can never find the skip button," and that was it. We moved on to arguing about which state has the most coastline.

Walking home, the happiness turned into guilt. It came in fast, like swimming into a cold spot in a lake. I kept thinking about the crew at the Owl, two hours ahead, probably finishing up around the time we sat down. It felt like cheating. Like I'd gone out and replaced them with a new booth and a new team name, and they didn't even know.

When I got home I reread "Thursday" from my second week here. The one where the homesickness hit so hard I had to hold onto the counter, and I wrote that nobody here would notice if I didn't show up anywhere. Tonight, if I hadn't shown up, Dev and Sam would have noticed. Sam saved me a seat. Lou knows our team name, sort of.

I also wrote back then that I wanted to remember what it felt like, so that later I could see how far I'd come. I don't think I've come all that far. But reading it tonight, I'm not the same guy who ate cereal for dinner and held onto the counter. Or I am, but now he has somewhere to be on Thursdays.

I sent a photo of our scoresheet to the group chat with "dead last, new personal record." Jason replied in about four seconds: "THIS IS THE BEST THING YOU'VE EVER SENT." Kat said I'm officially a two-trivia man now. Nobody felt replaced. I think maybe that was just me.
