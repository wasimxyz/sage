---
title: Lopsided
date: 2026-10-06
---

I went. I actually went inside this time. Walked in at 7:02, two minutes late on purpose so I wouldn't have to stand around before it started. That backfired, because everyone was already sitting down and they all turned to look at me.

The instructor is a retired shop teacher named Walt who has clay permanently under his fingernails and calls everyone "chief." He put me on the wheel next to another first-timer, Dev, who moved here from Austin in June for a job at one of the hospitals. Within ten minutes we'd both wrecked our first lump of clay. His flew off the wheel and hit the wall. Mine collapsed into a sad pancake. Walt said "good, now you know what not to do," and we both lost it.

Centering the clay is the hard part. You brace your elbows on your knees and push into it, steady, and if your mind wanders for even a second it goes off-center and starts to wobble. Walt said most people fight it, and you're not supposed to. You're supposed to stay patient with it until it settles. I'm not going to turn that into a whole life lesson, but I did think about it on the walk home.

By the end I'd made one bowl. It's lopsided, one side is way thicker than the other, and there's a thumbprint on the rim I couldn't get out. Walt said it has to dry and then get fired, so I can't take it home until next week. Which means I have to go back next week, which might have been his plan all along.

What I keep coming back to is that for about an hour and a half, I didn't check my phone once. I didn't think about the trivia photo, or Marissa, or whether the onboarding flow matters. My hands were busy and my head was quiet, the good kind of quiet, not the apartment kind. It reminded me of Saturdays at the woodshop. I forgot I used to feel like that.

Dev and I walked out together, and he asked if I was coming back next week. I said probably. He said "cool, I'll save you a wheel," like it was nothing. It kind of wasn't nothing.

On the walk home I called Jason. It was almost 11 in Chicago and it went to voicemail. I left a long, rambling message about Walt and the sad pancake and how sorry I am for being a ghost. Then I said "anyway, call me back," hung up, and felt like an idiot, but the good kind.

The bowl is ugly. I really want to see how it turns out.
