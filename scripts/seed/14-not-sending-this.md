---
title: Not sending this
date: 2026-10-01
---

Kat from trivia once said that when she can't figure out how she feels about someone, she writes them a letter she's never going to send. I've been stuck on Marissa since the trivia photo, so I'm trying it. I'm not sending this. I'm writing that down so I don't get any ideas at 1am.

Marissa,

It's been almost a year. I saw the photo from trivia last week. You looked happy, and I'm glad. I mean that, even if the rest of this letter makes it sound like I don't.

You were right about us. I want to say that first. You said you'd felt like my roommate for a long time, and you were right. You'd been asking me about the future for a year, about getting engaged, about buying a place, and every time I said "after this project." I knew what I was doing, even if I didn't admit it. I kept the door half open so I'd never have to walk through it or close it. I'm sorry. You deserved someone who would just answer.

But here's the thing I didn't know until I started writing this. I'm angry at you. Not for leaving. For how it went. You got to be the one who decided. You sat on the edge of our bed and told me how I felt, "I think you feel the same way," like you'd already figured me out and the conversation was just a formality. Maybe you had. But I never got to say anything that mattered, because you'd already said it for both of us.

And then you kept everything. You kept the table. I know I said yes when you asked, and I'd say yes again, but you kept the thing I made. You kept Elena's Sunday dinners. You kept the Owl. You kept Jason and Leah and Kat and Omar, or at least you kept Thursdays with them. I'm the one who had to move 2,000 miles to stop running into our whole life on every corner. You left and still got to stay. I stayed and still lost all of it.

I know that isn't fair. Nobody made me move. I said yes to that recruiter in a week because I wanted to run, and that was my choice, not yours. I know your friends were your friends first. But I'm writing it down because apparently it's been sitting in my chest for a year, and I didn't even know it was there.

I also miss you. That's the other thing I found while writing this. I miss how you hummed while you cooked and didn't know you were doing it. I miss you making me carry the heavy bag at the farmers market. I miss the coaster under the table leg. I miss being known by someone without having to explain myself first.

I told my journal a few weeks ago that I don't want you back. I'm less sure, writing this. I don't think I want us back, the polite version we turned into. But I don't know what to do with the part of me that still turns around when someone in a coffee shop laughs like you.

I hope you're happy. I hope it's been easier for you than it's been for me. I hope that's not a mean thing to say.

Ben

Okay. I read it back, and I feel shaky and a little sick, but also lighter. I didn't know about the anger. I really thought I was just sad. It's strange to find out something about yourself by writing it down.
