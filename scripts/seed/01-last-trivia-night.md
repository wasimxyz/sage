---
title: Last trivia night
date: 2026-07-30
---

Last Thursday at the Owl. Jason reserved the corner booth, which you can't actually reserve, so he just showed up at five and sat there for two hours guarding it with a basket of fries. Leah made a banner out of printer paper that said GOOD LUCK BEN in bubble letters, and Omar and Kat brought a card everyone had signed. We came in third. Of course we came in third. We've come in third almost every week for two years.

I was grateful the whole night, and I want to remember that part. These people showed up for me on a random Thursday. Kat gave a speech that was mostly about the time I got the capital of Australia wrong three weeks in a row. Jason kept putting his arm around my shoulders and then pretending he was just stretching. I've known him since we were eighteen and stuck in the same tiny dorm room, and I've never seen him try this hard not to look sad.

Marissa didn't come. I knew she probably wouldn't, but every time the door opened I looked up anyway. I think staying away was her being kind. She's still part of this group, maybe more than I am, since she's the one who remembers birthdays and plans everything. She could have come. She gave me the night instead.

Around eleven Jason walked me out to the curb and said, "You know you still have to come back for the wedding, right? Best man doesn't get to bail." He asked me a few months ago, at that same booth, two weeks before the recruiter emailed me. I said obviously, and he hugged me for a long time, and I said something dumb about how Seattle is only a four-hour flight. I'm going to miss him more than I know how to say.

And then the weird part. When the car pulled away, the first thing I felt was relief. Not sadness. Relief, like I'd just set down something heavy. I don't understand that. I love these people. I've been dreading tonight for weeks because I didn't want it to end, and then it ended and I could finally breathe.

Maybe I'm just tired. I spent the whole night smiling and saying "yeah, it's a huge opportunity" and "no, I'm excited, honestly," while keeping one eye on the door in case she walked in. By the end my face actually hurt. I don't think I've let myself be sad about leaving in front of anyone, including Jason.

Nine days until the flight. The apartment is half boxes. I'm sitting on the floor because I already sold the couch, and the banner is taped to the wall where the TV used to be. I'm going to pack it last.
