---
title: Sunday, again
date: 2026-10-25
---

I talked to people all day today. I'm writing that down first because a month ago I wrote an entry called "Sunday" about going until 4pm without saying a word out loud.

This morning was the call with Jason. He's decided the wedding toast needs a slideshow, and I've decided it doesn't, and we argued about it for twenty minutes like it was the most important thing in the world. Then he asked about the engagement party, and I told him I still don't know. He said, "Okay. That's allowed." I didn't know I needed him to say that until he did.

After that I took my sketchbook to the coffee place on the corner. The barista's name is Jess, which I finally learned because she wrote it on my cup as a joke after two months of me never asking. I sat by the window for two hours and drew tables. Long ones, round ones, one with a bench. On every single one, I drew a short leg without meaning to, erased it, and then drew it back in.

Around noon Dev texted to ask if I wanted to go to the farmers market on Broadway with him and Sam. I almost said I had plans. I actually started typing it. Then I deleted it and said yes. Sam bought a loaf of bread the size of a toddler and made us take turns carrying it, and I ended up with the heavy bag of groceries and pretended it was a workout. For a second that felt so familiar I had to stop walking.

Mom called at her usual time. She said, "You sound busy, Benny," and she sounded so happy about it. I told her about the tables. She asked me to send her a picture of the sketches, so I did, and she wrote back "the one with the bench!!!" Three exclamation points, which for her is basically screaming.

Tonight I reread "Sunday" from four weeks ago. I'd written that I gave the table away in about four seconds, and that if someone asked what I was building toward, I wouldn't have an answer. Then I went further back and reread "First night": the air mattress, the panic, scrolling through my contacts with nobody here. So I counted tonight. There are three people in this city I could actually call now: Dev, Sam, and Walt, who gave me his number "in case the kiln explodes." Four, if you count Priya for work stuff.

I also reread "The last box," where I wrote that I felt like I was house-sitting for some guy with a good job and a nice apartment, waiting for him to come home. I don't feel like that tonight. I feel like I live here. Not like I've figured it out, but like this is actually my kitchen and my rain and my ugly bowl full of keys.

I'm less lonely. I want to be honest about how much less, though, because I don't want this to turn into a postcard. It's still quiet here most nights. I still eat at the counter. I still miss the Owl, and Elena's dinners, and some version of Marissa I can't quite name. Today was a good Sunday, and I know there will be bad ones again.

And I still don't know what I'm building toward. That question from four weeks ago is still open. The difference is that it doesn't feel like a long empty hallway anymore. It feels more like the sketchbook: a lot of drafts, some short legs, nothing finished yet.

Woodshop orientation in thirteen days. The tools are still on the counter. Jason says the slideshow is happening whether I like it or not.
