Jason posted a photo from trivia tonight. The whole crew squeezed into the corner booth at the Owl, holding up the answer sheet because they finally won. First place. They were stuck in third for basically all of last year, and they won without me.

Marissa was in it. End of the booth, laughing at something off camera, wearing the green sweater she always pulled out once it got cold. I zoomed in, which I'm not proud of. I don't know why I expected her not to be there. They were her friends too. Probably more hers than mine, if I'm honest, since she was the one who remembered birthdays and planned everything.

I'm not even mad at her. I'm glad they still have each other. It's more that the photo made something obvious that I'd been avoiding. The life I left didn't stop when I left. It just closed up over the spot where I used to be. Everyone's still there doing Thursdays, and I'm here refreshing a photo.

Then I scrolled up through my texts with Jason, and that part was worse. He's texted me five times since I moved. A link to a dumb video. "How's the rain." "Call me when you're free, want to hear about the new job." A screenshot of the Cubs score. "You alive?" I answered two of them, both with about four words.

So I can't really tell myself they forgot about me. Jason has been reaching out the whole time. I'm the one who went quiet. I think part of me was waiting until I had something good to report. A group of friends, a routine, a version of Seattle that sounded like a win. I never got there, so I never called.

I liked the photo. Typed "congrats!! finally" and deleted it. Typed "miss you guys" and deleted that too. Ended up not commenting anything.

It's 11pm and I'm still up. Chicago is two hours ahead, so by the time I feel brave enough to call anyone, everyone there is asleep.
