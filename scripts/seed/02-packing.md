---
title: Packing
date: 2026-08-05
---

Three days left. I made a list tonight because my brain won't hold anything else right now.

- **Keeping:** the good knife, the cast iron, the record player, Grandma's quilt, my woodworking tools (haven't opened that box since the breakup)
- **Sold:** the couch, the bike, the bookshelf, the TV, the bed frame
- **Curb:** the plant stand, and the lamp that only works if you hit it
- **Not sure:** the box of things Marissa left behind. A sweater, two books, and the mug from Sleeping Bear.

The box is the hard part. It's been in the hall closet since she moved out nine months ago. I kept meaning to text her about it and never did, because texting her meant she'd write back, and I didn't know what I wanted her to say. Now I either mail it to her, which feels like making a point, or drop it at Jason's and let him deal with it, which feels cowardly. I'll probably do the cowardly one.

The strange thing is that I'm not as sad as I thought I'd be. I've lived in this apartment for three years. We picked it together because of the kitchen window, and I've cooked a thousand dinners at that stove. I should be crying in every room. Mostly I'm just checking things off.

I feel guilty about that, like it says something bad about me. Jason teared up at trivia. Mom cried on the phone on Sunday. And I'm over here wrapping glasses in newspaper and feeling almost nothing. I keep wondering if that's normal, or if I've gone numb somewhere along the way and didn't notice.

Maybe it's because I've already been leaving for a while. Since she moved out, every room here has been half empty in a way that has nothing to do with boxes. The kitchen wall still has a scuff where the corner of our table used to bump the radiator. I walk past it every morning. For nine months this place has been a museum of the life we had, and I've been the guy who works the front desk.

The movers come Friday. My flight is Saturday morning. Jason's driving me to O'Hare and says he's going to play the worst music he can find the whole way, which is his way of saying he'll miss me.
