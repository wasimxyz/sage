title: First day
date: 2026-08-10
---

First day. The office is huge. Three floors, a cafeteria with a pho station, and a wall of screens showing how many people opened the app today. At the studio, where I worked for six years, there were nine of us in one room above a dentist's office, and Rosa's dog slept under my desk. Here I got a badge, a welcome tote bag, and a calendar invite for "Culture 101."

My manager is Priya. She's sharp and warm, and she clearly read my portfolio before I got there, which I didn't expect. She asked about the booking site I designed for the community clinic on Fullerton, and I talked about it for way too long. That was the best part of the day. For ten minutes I felt like someone who's good at his job.

I'm on the onboarding team. My first project is the sign-up flow, the first five screens people see after they download the budgeting app. Priya said a lot of people quit before they finish, so it's a big deal. It's a real problem and I want to solve it. I just don't know yet who I'm solving it for. At the studio I could walk down the street and eat at the bakery whose menu I'd designed.

I was proud walking in this morning. Senior product designer, on a real team, at a company my mom has actually heard of. I was also nervous enough that I changed my shirt twice, got there 40 minutes early, and walked around the block three times so I wouldn't be the first one in.

At lunch the team asked the usual questions. Why Seattle, do I have family here, am I settling in. I said "it was just the right time for a change" and "honestly, so far so good," and I laughed in the right places. I heard myself doing it and recognized it. It's the same voice I used after Marissa left, when people at the studio asked if I was okay. Upbeat, a little jokey, no details. The new guy who's fine.

I don't know if that's bad. Nobody wants the new guy's whole life story on day one. But it was strange how easily it came out, like I'd been practicing for a year.

Everyone was nice. I think this could work. I keep saying that to myself, so I'm writing it down: I think this could work.
