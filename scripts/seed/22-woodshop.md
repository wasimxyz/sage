---
title: Woodshop
date: 2026-10-20
---

Went back to pottery. I was nervous about seeing Dev after bailing on trivia, and I spent the whole walk over writing an apology in my head. When I got there, his jacket was on my stool again. He said, "There he is. The delay guy." I said sorry for flaking. He said, "Dude, it was one Thursday. You're coming this week, though." I said yes. That was the whole thing. I'd been dreading it for five days.

While we were trimming, I told Dev about the table I built in Chicago. I don't know why it came out. Walt said something about how clay is forgiving and wood isn't, and I said, "Wood remembers every mistake," and then I was telling the whole story. The white oak, the three months of Saturdays, the short leg.

It turns out Dev's roommate Sam is a member at a community woodshop in Georgetown. Dev said it has a full shop, lockers, all the big machines, and you can rent bench time by the month after a safety orientation. He texted Sam right there, Sam sent the link, and I signed up for the orientation on the bus home. It's two and a half weeks from now, on a Saturday.

I'm excited, and it's scaring me a little. My heart's been going fast all evening, not in a panicky way, more like the night before a trip. I keep opening the woodshop's website and looking at photos of the benches.

Tonight I reread "Sunday," the entry where I wrote about the table. Marissa calling the short leg "the signature." Me giving it away in about four seconds. My tools in a box in the closet, still taped shut. So I went and opened the box. Everything's in there. My chisels are a little rusty, and the plane needs a new blade.

I think the scary part is that the last time I built something, it was for a life that ended. I built that table thinking we'd eat at it for thirty years. If I build something here, it's like saying out loud that I'm staying. That this is my life now, not a waiting room.

I don't know what I'd build first. Maybe a table. Maybe something smaller, so it doesn't feel like so much. Either way, the tools are out of the box and sitting on the counter next to the bowl.
