---
title: One year
date: 2026-10-17
---

One year today. I woke up at 6 and knew what day it was before I even opened my eyes, the way you know you're sick before you're fully awake.

I spent the morning doing anything that wasn't thinking about it. Cleaned the bathroom. Went to the hardware store for picture hooks I don't need. Walked down to Lake Union and back, which took two hours, and the whole time I kept replaying that night. Her sitting on the edge of the bed in her work clothes, still holding her keys. The radiator clanking. Me saying "okay" like an idiot because I couldn't think of anything else.

At 2:40 she texted. "Hey. I know what today is. I've been thinking about you, and I hope Seattle is being good to you. You don't have to reply. I just didn't want today to go by without saying something."

I read it maybe twenty times. And I felt about four things at once, so strongly that I had to sit down on the steps outside a laundromat.

Jealous, first. That she can write something that calm. That she has Nate, and the Owl, and Elena's dinners, and a whole Saturday where she can think about me for a minute and then go back to her life. She gets to be the kind, grown-up one, checking in. I'm the one sitting on laundromat steps.

Relieved, too. Relieved that she's okay, that she isn't angry at me, and that I didn't imagine the whole five years. She remembered. Part of me had been scared she'd let today pass like any other Saturday, which would have meant it was only ever heavy on my side.

Angry. That one surprised me less than it would have before the letter. It's the same thing I wrote in the letter I didn't send: she left, and she's still the one who gets to decide how this goes. She decided we were over. Now she's deciding we can be the kind of exes who text on anniversaries. And "you don't have to reply" is nice, but it also means that if I don't, I'm the one being cold.

And under all of it, tender. That's the only word I can find. She remembered the date. She wrote "I've been thinking about you." I pictured her typing it, probably at the table I built, and I felt an ache I don't have a name for. Not wanting her back, exactly. More like missing the version of me who got to be loved by her.

I can't sort them out. They're all true at the same time, and every time I think one is the real one, another one shows up. I wrote three replies and deleted them. One was too long, one was too cold, and one just said "thank you" with a heart, and the heart felt like a lie.

I haven't answered. I'm going to sleep on it. Jason's call is tomorrow morning, and I already know he's going to ask, and I'm weirdly glad. For the first time in a long time, I actually want to tell him something hard instead of hiding it.
