---
title: The last box
date: 2026-08-29
---

Unpacked the last box today. It was the one marked "misc kitchen" that I'd been stepping over for almost three weeks, and it turned out to be a colander, two mugs, and a waffle iron I've used twice. I put everything away, broke down the box, and then stood in the middle of the apartment with nothing left to do. It was 4pm on a Saturday.

The apartment is nice. Nicer than anything I could afford in Chicago: big window, wood floors, and if I lean left I can see that sliver of water. But it's so quiet. In Logan Square there was always something going on. The Blue Line, the guy downstairs practicing trumpet, somebody yelling about a parking spot. Here I can hear the fridge hum. I've started leaving a podcast on just so there's a voice in the room.

I think the word for what I felt standing there is lonely. I've been avoiding it because it sounds dramatic, but that's what it is. Unpacking gave me something to do every evening for three weeks. As long as there were boxes, I was "still moving in," and being alone was temporary. Now the boxes are gone, and it's just me, a Saturday afternoon, and nobody nearby to call.

I went for a walk around Capitol Hill to "explore," which mostly meant walking until I felt weird about walking, then getting a coffee so I'd have a reason to be somewhere. The place on the corner of my block is good. The barista remembered my order on my third visit, which makes her the person in this city who knows the most about me. That's a sad sentence, but it's true. There's a flyer by the register for a Tuesday night pottery drop-in. I took a picture of it and then felt dumb for taking a picture of it.

Jason texted this morning asking how the new place is. I typed out a long reply about the quiet and the window, and how weird it is to eat dinner without anyone asking about my day. Then I deleted it and sent "good man, still settling in." He sent a thumbs up. I didn't want to be the friend who moves across the country and immediately calls to complain. I meant to call him tonight. I'll call him tomorrow.

Everyone says the first few months in a new city are hard, and then one day it clicks. I believe them, I think. But right now it feels like I'm a guest in my own life. Like I'm house-sitting for some guy with a good job and a nice apartment, and I'm just waiting for him to come home.

Going to bed early. Tomorrow: groceries, call Jason, maybe find a gym.
