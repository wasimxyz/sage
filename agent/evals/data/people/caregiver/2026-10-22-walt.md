Group again. Walt was quiet for most of it. Near the end Pat asked if anyone wanted to say one thing they hadn't said out loud, and Walt, who rarely says more than a word, said, "Some nights I pray she goes in her sleep. I'd hold her hand while she did. I don't know what that makes me." The room went silent the way rooms do. Bernadette took his hand. Pat said, "It makes you someone who loves her and is tired." Walt said, "Maybe."

I sat on my hands. It didn't shock me. What shocked me is that it didn't shock me. I looked at Walt and could see exactly how a person gets there, how love and exhaustion share one body. I thought of Mom asleep at home with Yolanda in the chair, and what I felt was a fierce, ugly protectiveness. Not that. Not for her. I want her here. I want the woman she used to be, but I want this one here too, with the lotion and the toast.

On the drive home I called Neil, got voicemail, and hung up. I don't know what I'd have said. I think I wanted to hear someone say my name.
