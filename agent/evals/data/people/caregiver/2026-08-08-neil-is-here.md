title: Neil is here
---

Neil arrived yesterday at noon with a bouquet and a bag of bagels from the airport, which I want to be gracious about. He fixed the porch light. He took Mom to the park, and she was radiant. She told the ducks his name. He kept saying, "She seems great to me, honestly. Are you sure it's as bad as you say?" Not meanly. Like he was offering me good news.

Here's what I wanted to say, and didn't: she's great because you're here for forty-eight hours, because you're the visitor, because performing for company is the last thing she can still do. She'll be up at three in the morning next week asking where her mother is, and you'll be in Denver.

What I said was, "She has good days." He nodded like I'd conceded. I washed the same pan three times. I'm aware of how this reads. Resentment, boundaries, unmet needs, I could write the worksheet. What I can't write is the part where I'm also jealous of how easy he is with her, because he gets to be the one she's delighted by, and I get to be the one she puts up with.
