title: Packing a bag
---

Up at three. She was in the hall with her purse and a cardigan on inside out, waiting to go home. Her home is the house on Delaney Street, which was sold in 2019. This is the third night this week. I know the advice: don't argue, don't reorient too hard, offer tea. I offered tea. She said, "I don't want to be a bother." The thing about it is she was polite the whole time. It would be easier if she were awful.

We sat in the kitchen until five. She told me about a dance in 1963, in detail, a green dress, a boy who stepped on her foot. I haven't heard that story before. Whatever else is happening to her, she still has an entire evening in 1963, and I don't have anything like that.

She fell asleep on the couch. I watched her breathe until it was light, doing the math on the day: Yolanda at eight-thirty, the clinic at nine, I'll be late and useless. I'm so tired I can feel my teeth. At some point I started drafting an email to Neil in my head and had to stop, because it was all capital letters.
