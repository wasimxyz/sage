title: Group, the first time
---

Went to the Thursday caregivers' group at the hospital annex. Yolanda stayed late. I walked in prepared to dislike it and I'm happy to report that I did. Folding chairs in a circle, a woman with a clipboard (Pat, a social worker, who is lovely, which is annoying), a plate of grocery-store cookies. We did a check-in, one word for how you're feeling. Eight people said "tired." I said "fine," which I know is a dodge, and I know Pat knew, because she did the nod. The therapist nod. I'm fluent.

They have a script. "I'm noticing." "That sounds like a lot to hold." I know every line. I did years of weekly sessions and a good part of my adult vocabulary came from them. It was strange to hear it from the other side of the circle, aimed at a man named Walt whose wife doesn't know their kids anymore.

And yet. Bernadette, whose husband has Parkinson's dementia, told us she hides snacks in the garage so she can eat something nobody needs to share. I laughed, then recognized myself, and then I wanted to leave. I stayed. I'll go again, probably out of spite.
