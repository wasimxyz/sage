date: 2026-07-03
title: Iris is here
---

Iris was born yesterday at 4:12 in the morning, after nineteen hours that I'm going to tell the whole story of eventually, but right now I'll just say that at hour fourteen I yelled at a very nice nurse about ice chips, and she said, "You can have all the ice chips you want," in the voice of someone who has been yelled at about ice chips for thirty years, and I felt like a monster, and then I felt nothing, because the next one hit.

Kit cried before I did. Kit's hands were shaking so badly they couldn't cut the cord at first and the doctor said take your time and Kit said I don't have time, I mean, I have all the time, and everybody laughed, and I laughed and then I was crying anyway. Everyone says you meet this tiny person and the world rearranges, and what I actually thought was oh, she's real, you're a real person, you've been in there the whole time. She had hair stuck sideways like she'd been in a windstorm. She looked at the lights like she was annoyed about them.

We settled on the name by the window around six, once the room went quiet. Iris. Margot is the middle name, which was Kit's idea, and I might have cried about that too.

They kept us two nights. The food was bad and I ate all of it. A man came in on the second day to do the car seat check and I could not tell you what he said, I nodded for ten minutes, I think he told us the straps were too loose, I think I said thank you. Kit was the one who listened. At one point I looked down and Iris had a fist around my finger and I thought I'll never be able to take this finger back, which is a strange thing to think and I think probably normal.

I'm writing this at 3 a.m. with Iris on my chest, which Kit says I'm not supposed to do, the sleeping-on-chest thing, and so I'm awake on purpose, on guard. I've been staring at her for forty minutes. Her breath makes a tiny whistle. I'm going to remember this, I told myself. The whistle, the smell. I am so tired I could cry and so happy I might too.
