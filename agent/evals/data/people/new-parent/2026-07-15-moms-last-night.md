date: 2026-07-15
title: Mom's last night
---

Mom leaves tomorrow morning and I've been trying not to think about it, and then thinking about it more, which is a skill. She's been here since Sunday and in that time she has refolded every towel in the house, cleaned a part of the oven I didn't know existed, and made a lasagna the size of a suitcase that is now in the freezer in portions, labeled in her handwriting, "IRIS'S PARENTS," as if we're a species. I've been so grateful and also so irritated that I've gotten a little dizzy. She rearranged the kitchen and now the bottle brush is on the wrong side of the sink. I said nothing. I've let a lot of things go this week, which is either growth or sleep deprivation.

Tonight after Kit took Iris upstairs we sat at the table with decaf and she told me about when I was a baby. I didn't know most of it. I had colic, apparently, for eleven weeks, six to nine every single night, screaming like something was wrong with the world, and she walked me up and down the hallway until there was a groove in the carpet, and eventually she was crying too, the two of us wailing in the hallway. She told it laughing. She said, "I could have strangled you, honest to God. I loved you so much I thought my teeth would crack, and I could have strangled you." And she wiped her eyes with the dish towel and said, "That's parenting. That's the whole thing, right there."

I laughed because it was funny the way she told it, and because it's very her. She says things like that. She said the same thing about my driving test. Then she patted my hand. Then she said, "You're doing really well, you know." I said I was just surviving. She said, "That's the job."

I'm going to miss her. I didn't think I'd say that. I'm going to miss the lasagna and the folded towels and the way she says "okay, my loves" to Iris like she's practiced it for years. Okay, I'm going to cry now. Kit's calling. Iris is hungry.
