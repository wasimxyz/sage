title: Mom again
---

Mom's back. She drove the whole way from Ohio in one go, with a cooler on the passenger seat, which she says is for the "good cheese" but I think is mostly to prove she can. She's here through next Saturday morning to cover the week when I go back to work and before the daycare starts, and I'm trying to remember to be grateful in the right order.

Within four hours she'd found the bathroom. I mean she's been in it, of course, but she looked at the tub and said, "You haven't been bathing her, have you? Kit does it." I said I've got a thing with my wrist. She said, "Oh, wrists. Don't talk to me about wrists. I had De Quervain's when you were a baby, I couldn't open a jar for six months," and then she told me about the jar for ten minutes, which saved me. She did not push. I think she sees more than she says. She watched me for a bit afterward, over her tea.

I'm tired tonight in the way where I can feel my eyelashes. Iris adores her. Mom does a voice for her, a high squeaky one, and Iris laughs, really laughs, and Mom looks over at me, glowing, and I look back and I don't mean to but I laugh too. It's quite something to watch your mother get to be a grandmother. It doesn't make me feel like a grown-up. It makes me feel eleven.
