title: Bath night
---

Bath night. Kit does them. I've been telling Kit it's my wrist, that I have a thing with my wrist from all the holding, which isn't entirely a lie because my wrist does hurt, but it's not why. I stand in the doorway of the bathroom with a towel over my shoulder while Kit does the baby tub, and the warm water, and the little cup, and Iris kicking and furious and delighted, and I watch every second of it like it's a heist.

Here's why. The first time I tried it alone, week three, I had her slick and wet in my arms and I was leaning over the tub and the thought came, the same kind of thought as the stairs: what if she slips out and goes under. Not a wish. It isn't even a thought exactly, it's more of a flash, with sound, and my stomach dropped to my feet. I put her in the tub and held her with both hands and my whole body shaking, and I got through it, and I dried her off and dressed her and handed her to Kit and said I felt sick. Kit felt my forehead. I haven't done one since.

I know it isn't rational. I read about it at four in the morning, the way you do. Apparently it's a thing. It's common, a lot of new parents get them, the pictures, the awful what-ifs, and they make you avoid things. That's what the pages said. I read it three times. It helped for about a minute, and then I was lying there working out how long I could keep avoiding bath time. Until she's eighteen? She'll start doing it herself by then, probably.

I'm not going to tell Kit that. Not yet. I'll say my wrist is better when it's better. Now Kit's wrapping her in the hooded towel, the one with the ears, she looks like a small bear, and she's looking right at me over Kit's shoulder with that look. She trusts me. I don't know what to do with that.
