date: 2026-10-03
title: Told Kit
---

I told Kit. This morning, in the kitchen, while Iris was in the bouncy seat staring at the ceiling fan. I said, "I need to tell you something and I need you to not do the face," and Kit said "what face," and did the face, and I said, that one, and Kit stopped.

I told them about the stairs, and the baths, and why it isn't my wrist. I said all of it. It took about four minutes and I looked at the floor for three of them. I said I didn't want it, that I'd never, that it makes me feel sick, and that I'd stopped doing baths because I couldn't stand it. I said it kept happening and I didn't know why. When I finally looked up Kit's eyes were wet, which I wasn't ready for, and Kit said, "Oh. Oh, honey." Kit put their arms around me and Iris cooed from the bouncy seat as though she'd been invited.

Kit said a lot of kind things. I won't remember most of them, but one was, "That sounds exhausting. Carrying that by yourself." And one was, "You'd never." Which I know. I know I'd never. But it was so different hearing it from somebody else. And Kit asked, carefully, whether I wanted to talk to someone, a person, not just them. I said maybe. Kit said they'd already looked, last week, because they'd noticed me not doing baths and thought something was up and didn't want to push. So there's a number now, for a postpartum support line, on a sticky note on the fridge under the pizza magnet. I haven't called. I've looked at it twice.

Kit also said, "It's okay if the baths stay mine for a while." I said I'd like to try again eventually. I don't know when. Not this week.

Here's the thing I can't say to anyone: I'm still scared. Telling didn't make the pictures stop. This afternoon on the stairs it happened again, a little one, a flicker, and I just held on and went down. But I wasn't alone with it for the first time. Then Iris threw up on both of us and Kit said, "Well, that's one way to end a heart-to-heart," and we changed our shirts.
