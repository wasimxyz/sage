title: Four months
---

Five hours. Five hours in a row, last night, from 9:40 to 2:40, and I woke up before she did, in the dark, in total panic, and ran into the nursery, and she was just lying there with her eyes open in the glow of the nightlight, not even crying, looking at the ceiling like a small philosopher, and I stood there and wanted to weep from gratitude. I did my math anyway. Five hours and then two more hours in two pieces. Seven. Seven hours. I haven't had seven hours since June. I feel like a person who's been unplugged and plugged back in, a little buzzy and unsure which end is which.

She's four months and eleven days. I know exactly, I count. She laughs now, not at jokes but at specific things: Kit's sneeze, the sound of the faucet, a dog on the street with one big floppy ear. She tries to eat my chin. She's figured out that if she grabs my hair I'll turn my head, and so she grabs it a lot, to make me turn. It's the first experiment I've seen her do. I told Kit, "She's doing science," and Kit said, "She's four months old," and I said "exactly."

I don't know how to explain what's different, because most things aren't. The thoughts still come, less often, quieter. This week it was once in the car, a flash of the intersection, and I breathed through it and drove. The support line called back on Thursday and I talked to a woman for half an hour, and she said what the website said, only slower, and that helped more than I expected. I told her about the baths. She said a lot of parents feel this and the thing is not to fight the picture. I'm trying. I'm bad at not fighting. We have another call next week.

Work is okay. Tamsin and I got lunch and she asked about Iris and I took out my phone and then realized I'd been showing her pictures for eleven minutes. She was very kind about it. Dennis brought donuts. Kit's folding laundry in the next room, humming, off-key. I'm going to try to sleep before the next one hits, and I'm writing this instead, which is very much the old me. Five hours.
